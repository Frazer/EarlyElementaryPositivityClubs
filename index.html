Kids Positivity Club ☀️

A joyful 60-minute club for early elementary children, combining music, movement, mindfulness, realistic affirmations, gratitude and kindness.

Notice the good. Create more good.

60-minute club agenda

Keep the rhythm moving: energetic → thoughtful → active → calm. Young children usually engage better when seated discussion is broken up with frequent movement and singing.

0–5 min

Welcome circle + “One Good Thing”

Everyone shares one good thing from their day, week, or life. Passing is always allowed.

5–12 min

Sing-along + movement

Start with familiar, participatory songs. Add gestures, clapping, dancing or partner actions.

12–18 min

What are affirmations?

Introduce positive self-talk as practice for the brain. Use short, believable, action-oriented statements.

18–25 min

Positivity game

Play Affirmation Ball, Kindness Detective, Turn It Around, or Gratitude Popcorn.

25–32 min

Statues → Mindful Statues

Dance, freeze, then notice feet, heartbeat, breathing and sounds before starting again.

32–38 min

Mindfulness game

Try Bell Detective, Spidey Senses, mindful walking, a sound map or a breathing buddy.

38–45 min

Dance + song reset

Bring the energy back up with a favorite upbeat song and lots of movement.

45–52 min

Kindness + gratitude

Notice helpful actions, thank someone, or invent one small way to improve another person’s day.

52–57 min

Guided calm

Take a few slow breaths and imagine handling something difficult with calmness, courage and kindness.

57–60 min

Closing affirmation + cheer

Finish together with the club motto, a compassionate wish and one enormous cheer.

Positivity + affirmation games

🏐

Affirmation Ball

Toss a soft ball around the circle. The catcher completes one prompt: “I am good at…”, “I am learning…”, “I helped someone by…”, or “Something I like about myself is…”

🦸

Superpower Affirmations

Invent a psychological superpower—Brave Brain, Kindness Power, Calm Power, Try-Again Power or Curiosity Power—then strike a superhero pose while saying it.

🔄

Turn It Around

Transform a discouraging thought into something truthful and useful. “I can’t draw this” becomes “I can’t draw it yet, but I can practice.”

🕵️

Kindness Detective

Look for someone being helpful, patient, brave, friendly or thoughtful. Describe exactly what they did.

💬

Compliment Circle

Say something genuinely positive about another child’s effort, kindness, persistence or actions—not just appearance or talent.

🍿

Gratitude Popcorn

Rapid-fire things we appreciate: people, animals, abilities, nature, food, experiences, funny moments and little acts of care.

Mindfulness games

The aim is not to force children to become quiet. It is to help them practice noticing attention, body sensations, thoughts and feelings without immediately reacting.

🗿

Mindful Statues

Dance while music plays. Freeze when it stops. While frozen, notice: feet, heartbeat, breathing and the quietest sound you can hear.

🔔

Bell Detective

Ring a bell or chime. Children listen carefully and raise a hand when they can no longer hear it.

🕷️

Spidey Senses

For 30 seconds, become a noticing superhero: find 3 things you see, 2 things you hear and 1 body sensation.

👣

Mindful Walking

Walk absurdly slowly and feel heel → foot → toes. Then try astronaut, ninja or giant walking while staying attentive.

🧸

Breathing Buddy

Lie down with a stuffed animal on the belly and watch it gently rise and fall with each breath.

🍓

Mindful Eating

Examine one raisin, berry or cracker as if you are an alien scientist. Notice appearance, smell, texture and taste slowly.

👂

Sound Map

Close eyes for 30 seconds and count or remember as many different sounds as possible.

🌦️

Emotion Weather

“If your feelings were weather right now, what would they be?” Sunny, windy, stormy, foggy… then remember: weather changes, and feelings do too.

How to teach affirmations well

“Your brain learns from what you repeatedly practice. Thoughts are a kind of practice.”

Positive self-talk is most useful when it supports reality, agency and action. Children do not need to pretend every situation is wonderful.

✓ Better

Believable, flexible and connected to choices:

“I can learn difficult things.”
“I can ask for help.”
“I can calm my body.”
“I can try again.”

△ Less useful

Grand claims a child may not believe:

“I am the greatest at everything.”

Optimism works better when it builds confidence and keeps children connected to reality.

I can learn difficult things.
I can try again.
I can calm my body.
I can ask for help.
I can choose kindness.
Mistakes help my brain learn.
I can make someone’s day better.
I am becoming better through practice.

Closing ritual 🌍

“May I be happy.
May you be happy.
May everyone be happy.
And may we help make it so.”

Finish with one slow breath, the motto “Notice the good. Create more good.” and a giant group cheer.
Made for joyful, compassionate, reality-based early elementary positivity clubs.
Tip: adapt timing, language and sensory demands to the children in front of you. Participation in sharing, touch and eyes-closed activities should always be optional.
